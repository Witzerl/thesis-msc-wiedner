## 0. What this project asks, and how the document is organised

**The question is "what does a model need in order to predict GA progression,
and what framework does it need to sit in?" — not "can a graph PDE solver do
this?".** The project began as an adaptation of two specific neural-PDE-solver
frameworks (MP-PDE and MM-PDE, §5) because the clinical partner suggested them,
and that adaptation is still the largest single piece of engineering here. But
what it produced is a *fixed experimental framework with one swappable slot*,
and the result the thesis reports is a survey through that slot rather than a
verdict on any one architecture. §5 and §5b should be read as two halves of one
chapter, not as a subject and its appendix.

**What is fixed for every arm** (this is the framework, and it is the part that
demonstrably transfers): one visit in, no history; an 11-channel state on the
`49×1024` en-face grid; a $\Delta t$-conditioned autoregressive residual
operator $u_{t+\Delta t} = u_t + \Delta t \cdot f_\theta$ evaluated on all
channels, with a zero-initialised head so every model starts at exact
persistence; the time-budgeted pushforward curriculum; the channel-weighted MSE
+ soft-Dice objective; per-module gradient clipping; the same optimiser, epoch
budget, folds, seeds, evaluation, model selection and replicate floor. The
comparison is controlled because **only $f_\theta$ changes** — the loss,
curriculum, evaluation and bookkeeping live in one shared pipeline that a
backbone module is forbidden to duplicate (§5b).

**What varies is $f_\theta$,** selected by `--backbone` and optionally wrapped
by `--integrator`: a message-passing GNN on a k-NN graph (§5), the same GNN on a
dilated physical-scale stencil (the locked model), a U-Net, an FNO, an FNO with
a local-kernel bypass, a graph U-Net, a Finite Element Network with a learned
transport term, a per-pixel model with no spatial context at all, and a
fixed-step Runge–Kutta integration of any of them (§5b).
Nine settings of that slot, spanning six architecture classes, all 5-fold and
all reported the same way. Every one is parameter-matched to within **1.25×**
of the locked model's 67,147 — U-Net w5 1.24×, FNO 1.18×, localized-kernel FNO
1.19×, graph U-Net 1.08×, FEN 1.02×, k-NN GNN 1.12×, and the RK4 wrapper adds
no parameters at all — except the two *deliberate* controls, the 44×-capacity
U-Net and the 0.22× per-pixel floor, which exist precisely to bound the
parameter-count question from both sides.

**The headline is that the framework transfers and the architecture class does
not decide the outcome.** The three strongest arms — the dilated-stencil GNN
(0.5258), the parameter-matched U-Net (0.5046) and the T-FEN (0.5337) — are
statistically **indistinguishable** from one another at the available precision,
while a per-pixel model with no spatial context is bit-exactly persistence
(0.0000). What separates arms is therefore not the operator class but which
*ingredients* they carry, and those are measurable one at a time:

| ingredient | measured effect | status |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial context at all | 0 message-passing layers = **0.0000**, exactly persistence, against 0.4529 for one ring (T8) | required, trivially |
| Physical reach at lesion scale (~0.12 mm/hop) | **+0.074 ± 0.008**, 5/5 folds, 74/75 eyes, replicated at a second seed, at 0.89× the parameters (T7i/T7j) | **established** — the largest effect in the project |
| Global context *and* local detail together | a global-only operator (FNO) is established *below* the U-Net at the anchor; adding a 3×3 local bypass (+800 params) brings it level with the U-Net and to **+0.047 ± 0.016 over the k-NN GNN on 5/5 folds** — the only arm to win every fold (T7c/T7d) | **established** |
| An explicit transport (advection) term | **+0.053 ± 0.007**, 5/5 folds, at both seeds (T8f) — and it recovers the near-term growth bin to the dilated stencil's own value | **established at equal width**, with two caveats that must travel with it (§5b): the control has **half the parameters**, and the learned velocity is not quotable |
| Parameter count | a 44× U-Net *loses* to the parameter-matched one (T7b); GNN depth/width/degree all null | **not** a factor |
| Mesh adaptation (the MM-PDE half) | dual − parameter-matched bypass = **−0.0001 ± 0.0060**, the tightest null here (T4), at ~10× the cost | **no** measurable contribution |
| Time-integration order | RK4 − Euler is null over both a local graph operator and a global spectral one (T8n/T9/T9b), at ~4.8× the cost | **not** a factor |
| Normalisation, aggregation, edge-direction features | every GNN-internal knob under its floor (T7e–T7g) | **not** a factor |
| Patient covariates, learned coefficient surrogate | null (T6), null at every width (T5) | **not** a factor |

Two of those rows are the applied contribution and the rest are the survey:
reach and transport are two independent routes to the *same* deficit — the GA
front moves ~10–25 fine-axis pixels per clinical visit, further than any
one-hop stencil can see — and two architecturally unrelated constructions
correct it to the same place. That coincidence, not any single arm's score, is
the project's central architectural result.

**Where each thing is written down.** §1 is the data; §2 the mesh mover; §3 the
time conditioning and the dual-branch composition; §4 the coefficient surrogate;
**§5 the graph-solver adaptation** (the MP-PDE/MM-PDE half, including the graph
construction that turned out to matter most); **§5b the rest of the backbone
family** on the same terms; §6 the evaluation and the full arm table; §7 known
issues. Sections 5 and 5b carry equal weight by design.

## 1. Dataset & Clinical Alignment

- **Source:** Medical University of Vienna (MUW) Geographic Atrophy (GA) dataset.
- **Coordinate Systems:** Global (SLO/FAF images) and OCT (rectangle on SLO). Affine transforms map between them.
- **Grid & Spacing:**
  - Native OCT acquisition: a Heidelberg volume of (n B-scans, 496 axial, n A-scans) covering the ~6×6 mm macular region. Both lateral counts vary per eye — **38–66 B-scans × 961–1719 A-scans across the 553 cohort visits, 67 distinct shapes** — because the raw scans were resampled upstream to one constant gold-standard spacing, so pixel *spacing* is treated as exact and constant across the cohort while pixel *counts* differ. No visit is natively `49 x 1024`; that is the modelling grid (next bullet). **Open caveat:** the constant-spacing assumption has only been checked against the cohort's own recorded transforms, where it holds to ~2 % (NOTES F10.14, still unverified — settling it needs the DICOM spacing fields). Nothing in the model depends on it, but every area reported in mm² does, so the Methods chapter should state it as an assumption rather than a measurement.
  - En-face projection shape used for modeling: `49 x 1024`, reached by symmetric **center-crop or zero-pad, never resampling** — which is what keeps the pixel spacing exact and identical across the cohort. The fixed physical window is $L_x \times L_y = 49 \cdot 0.12118 \times 1024 \cdot 0.00568 \approx 5.938 \times 5.816$ mm. **Note:** Unlike standard PDE toy datasets (e.g., SWE) which use quadratic/square domains, this framework operates on a highly asymmetrical rectangular domain.
  - **The crop is not lossless, and the Methods chapter must say so.** Measured over all 553 visits: 149 are row-padded and 115 column-padded; the window cuts real lesion area in **27.3 %** of visits (more than 5 % of the lesion in 6.0 %, worst case 25.7 %); and **31.1 %** of cropped lesions touch the crop border. Growth across that border is *censored with no missingness flag*, so training supervises on censored truth and the @360d change-region metrics under-measure progression for roughly one visit in five — which also skews comparability with whole-FOV literature numbers. The earlier rationale ("the pathology lies in the central macula, the discarded periphery is clinically irrelevant") is **false as measured** and must not reach the thesis text. The 49×1024 window was nevertheless kept deliberately: larger windows trade truncation for up to 35 % zero-pad and ~1.9× the node count, and ~19 % border-touch is irreducible because those lesions reach the scan's own edge. The consequences are handled downstream instead — a pad-aware border-touching vs interior split of the change-region metric, the pad-masked `_anat` variant (§6), and an optional `--exclude_pad_nodes` loss masking.
  - Gold standard spacing: `[0.12118, 0.003867, 0.00568]` (in mm).
- **State Representation (11 Channels):**
  - Channel 0: Binary GA Mask (disease segmentation).
  - Channels 1-10: Retinal layer boundaries. Retina thickness is calculated via `layers[...,-1] - layers[...,0]`.
- **Patient Covariates:** Age at each visit (`--age_mode per_visit`: baseline age + elapsed years, z-scored over all training `(eye, visit)` ages — what the canonical `*_agevisit` precomputes carry; the precompute's own argparse default is still the frozen baseline age) and Sex (categorical) are broadcast per-node and concatenated to the GNN's `variables` vector. **Measured (T6, 5 folds): they earn nothing.** Removing both reads $+0.0198 \pm 0.0140$ SE fold-paired (3/5 folds, and f4 carries ~60 % of it) and $+0.0264 \pm 0.0069$ SE per eye (54/75 eyes, $t = 3.85$, robust to leaving any one fold out) — no detectable benefit, with the residual pointing *against* them. That is not proven equivalence: the two instruments disagree in strength and both belong in the write-up. Baseline vs per-visit age is a clean null ($+0.0045 \pm 0.0042$). Both covariates nevertheless stay enabled in the locked recipe. The plausible mechanism is that they are two graph-level scalars broadcast to all 50,176 nodes and constant along an eye's trajectory, so they cannot localise anything and are trivially overfittable at 59 training eyes.

## 2. DMM training on the native OCT grid (revised 2026-08-04)

> **This section previously described training the DMM on a square 256² SLO mask
> and applying it at OCT resolution. That is no longer the solver's path.** The
> mesh-mover now trains *and* is applied on the native anisotropic `(49, 1024)`
> OCT grid. The original blocker was never the anisotropy — it was the unblurred
> binary mask's step gradients driving the Monge–Ampère chain to NaN, which the
> monitor Gaussian blur fixed a week before the 256² pivot. A 55-run native
> hyperparameter search produced a mesh that is good and valid on its own terms
> (0 tangled cells on every scored mask, seed and epoch), and the native path
> additionally removes the `49×1024 → 256² → 49×1024` round-trip and the
> prediction surrogate entirely. It is *not* claimed to beat the SLO path: the
> boundary-adaptation score is pool-dependent and no common-pool re-score of the
> two grids exists, so no native-vs-SLO ranking is supportable
> ([DMM_HPSEARCH_NATIVE.md](GraphPDE/mesh/demos & explanations/DMM_HPSEARCH_NATIVE.md)).
> The SLO description below is retained as the historical record of how the DMM
> was first made to train, and the DMM-side SLO code remains supported so those
> runs stay reproducible.

### 2.1 The original SLO-mask formulation (historical)

The DMM is **trained on the high-resolution Scanning-Laser-Ophthalmoscopy
(SLO) mask cropped to the OCT field-of-view**, not on the OCT volume
directly. This decouples training-time grid (square, isotropic) from
inference-time grid (rectangular, the OCT lateral sampling) and removes the
genuinely asymmetry-specific workarounds the OCT-direct path required — though
two features introduced under that heading turned out not to be removable (see
"Why this design" below).

**Two grids, one continuous mesh.** The DMM is a DeepONet-style operator
(Lu, Jin, Pang, Zhang & Karniadakis, *Nature Machine Intelligence* 3:218–229,
2021) and therefore has two heads — the decomposition is unchanged on the
native path of §2.2, only the branch's input grid moves:

| Head | What it sees | At training | At MM-PDE inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branch CNN | the field $u(x)$ | $(1,\,1,\,256,\,256)$ SLO mask | same — `window["slo_mask"]` |
| Trunk MLP | continuous query $(\xi_1,\xi_2)\in[0,1]^2$ | random samples | OCT lateral grid, $(49,1024)$ flattened to $[0,1]^2$ |

The trunk is grid-agnostic; querying it on $(49,1024)$ at inference is
the same continuous function $\phi(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ that was sampled at
random coordinates during training. The branch is shape-locked to its
training-time input — hence the requirement that the SLO mask field
travels with each window through the MM-PDE pipeline.

**SLO mask construction** (`tools/precompute_graphs_ga.py:load_slo_mask_oct_fov`;
**corrected 2026-08-09, audit C1**). Per visit, each square output pixel is
mapped to fractional OCT en-face coordinates and through the recorded
affine `T_mask` (from `oct.transform.json`) into `mask_global.png` array
coordinates, where the mask is nearest-sampled — verified to reproduce
`mask_oct.png` at IoU ≥ 0.9995 (min 0.99957, median 1.0000) on all 553 cohort
visits.
The output samples the SLO mask at full row resolution rather than
replicating the OCT mask's ~51 native rows, which is the point of the SLO
representation.

> **History (audit C1).** The original two-stage construction — crop
> `mask_global.png` with the `transform_oct_fov` corners from
> `slo.transform.json`, then nearest-resize — was **mis-registered**: the
> corner axes were bound swapped, and the crop ignored the shift/rescale
> terms that `T_mask` carries (mean IoU 0.588, median 0.64, against the
> correct reconstruction; lesion centroids displaced by up to a quarter of
> the domain). The earlier "lossless crop" claim here was therefore false.
> Every `*_slo256` precompute built before 2026-08-09 carries the
> mis-registered `slo_mask` field; they are deliberately **not**
> regenerated — the SLO DMM path is deprecated (the solver consumes only
> native `--mask_source oct` checkpoints), and the historical SLO
> mesh-quality numbers remain *internally* valid (trained and scored
> against the same supplied mask). Thesis claims about the DMM should be
> scoped to the native path.

The resulting mask lives in $[0,1]$ (binary 0/1 float32), is single-
channel by construction, and is stored as a per-window field
`w["slo_mask"]` alongside the existing OCT-side state. The MM-PDE
backbone still consumes the full $(11,49,1024)$ OCT tensor; only the
DMM's branch sees the SLO mask.

**Monitor function on a single channel.** With one channel the
multi-channel Frobenius norm collapses to a plain gradient magnitude, and
the monitor — the density the mesh equidistributes, in the sense of Huang
& Russell, *Adaptive Moving Mesh Methods*, Springer 2011 — is
$$M = 1 + \log\!\Big(1 + \frac{\|\nabla u\|}{\alpha_{\text{scale}}\,\alpha + \epsilon}\Big),$$
where $u$ is the (Gaussian-blurred) mask. **The `log1p` compression is
live and load-bearing on both the SLO and the native path**
(`--monitor_use_log1p`, default on, and on in the canonical native
checkpoint): it bounds the peak-to-bulk ratio at the lesion ring so the
Monge–Ampère target stays achievable. It was briefly removed and restored
on 2026-05-11, and the native `NOLOG1P` ablation scores below an
unadapted mesh. The blur is likewise still applied — the binary 0/1 mask
has step-function gradients regardless of grid shape, and a small blur
keeps the monitor finite ($\sigma\!\sim\!2$ px in the first SLO run,
`SLO_BASELINE`; the SLO era later settled on $\sigma = 4$ px, and the
native grid on $\sigma = 1.0$ px — see §7).

**Why this design.** The OCT direct-training path carried several
workarounds that *were* asymmetry-specific — axis-asymmetric soft-NN
interpolation, and the mask-only Frobenius restriction that suppressed the
89 %-mass contribution of the quasi-static layer channels — and those do
dissolve on a square 256² single-channel mask. **Two others were
misclassified as anisotropy workarounds and should not be read that way.**
The log-compressed monitor was introduced to balance the 21:1 y/x gradient
mismatch but has a second, grid-independent purpose (bounding the
peak-to-bulk ratio); it was removed at the pivot and **restored on
2026-05-11**, and is required on both grids. Replicate padding of the blur
kernel was introduced to stop zeros bleeding across the 49-px short axis,
but zero-pad border bleed is likewise grid-independent — a lesion touching
the crop edge gets a phantom monitor ridge on an image boundary where no
lesion boundary exists. It was reverted at the pivot and restored
flag-gated in 2026-08-09 as `--monitor_mask_pad` (audit C4); the default is
still `zeros` and the flag has never been swept in training, so this one is
open rather than settled. The generalisable lesson, recorded in
[DMM_GA_DESIGN.md](GraphPDE/mesh/demos & explanations/DMM_GA_DESIGN.md):
"feature X was added for the anisotropy" is not sufficient evidence that X
can be dropped once the grid changes. The trade-off is the
4× downsample's loss of sub-pixel boundary precision — empirically
acceptable; the resulting mesh follows the lesion edge cleanly on all
representative eyes inspected.

### 2.2 The native-OCT-grid formulation (current — the solver's path)

**The native-OCT-grid path is now the solver's path (since 2026-08-04).** The
DMM is trained on the GA mask *on its own* $(49,1024)$ OCT grid, selected by
`dmm.py --mask_source oct`. (`dmm.py`'s own CLI default is still `slo`, so the
historical runs stay reproducible; every native run and every launcher passes
`--mask_source oct` explicitly.) This does **not** revive the removed
11-channel OCT-*tensor* regime: the input is still a single-channel binary
mask, just read from channel 0 of the OCT state instead of from `slo_mask`.

The premise it overturned: the native grid was abandoned because the
gradient-based monitor produced NaN on the anisotropic grid — but that
failure was the *unblurred* binary mask's step gradients, and the monitor
Gaussian blur (§2.1 above, added a week before the 256² pivot) fixes it.
The anisotropy was never the blocker. A physically-matched *anisotropic*
blur kernel — the obvious way to "correct" the ~21:1 pixel-spacing imbalance —
was tested and **refuted** (no net adaptation gain, $p = 0.95$, $n = 16$, and
it folded the mesh intermittently), as was a determinant-Jacobian barrier.
Both are kept as negative results and are deliberately not in the code path.

**What the native path bought.** A 55-run hyperparameter search on the full
478-mask cohort (`--train_split all`) produced the canonical checkpoint
`GA_NAT_O512x2_S1`, which reaches good, valid meshes — **0 tangled cells on
every scored mask, seed and epoch** — and removes the round-trip and the
prediction surrogate described below. Two caveats travel with those scores:
every one of the 55 runs trains on the full cohort, so the mesh metrics are
**in-sample**, and the winner has the *largest* seed spread in the search on the
head-16 pool (sd 0.058), tying `GA_NAT_ANCHOR` at 0.061 on the eye-spread pool. No
common-pool re-score of the native and SLO grids exists (the SLO runs were
deleted), and the boundary-adaptation score is pool-dependent, so **no
native-vs-SLO ranking is claimed**. Its recipe departs from
the SLO era in two coupled places — the deepened potential head
$[1024,512,512,1]$ and a *sharper* monitor blur $\sigma = 1.0$ px, which
interact strongly ($\sigma=1$ was worth $+0.003$ at the shallow head but
$+0.24$ at the deep one) — while the branch stays `pool`, one of the two
grid-agnostic variants (`pool` and `pool_tiny` are the only ones built on
`AdaptiveAvgPool2d`; every other variant sizes its flatten-to-`Linear` from
the training grid and is therefore locked to it).

Architecturally it also removes the $49\times1024 \to 256^2 \to 49\times1024$
round-trip that existed only to marshal the solver's predicted mask onto the
DMM's fixed 256² sensor grid, and with it the surrogate-mask distribution
mismatch at rollout steps $t>0$ ([ARCHITECTURE.md §12b](ARCHITECTURE.md)).
`MovingMeshHelper._state_to_native_mask`
now takes channel 0 of the state, denormalises it and thresholds it back to
binary: at $t_0$ the branch sees the real segmentation and during rollout it
sees the model's own prediction, so the deployment-honest input is automatic —
no resize, no surrogate. **`GraphPDE/train.py` therefore *requires*
`mask_source == 'oct'` and raises on an SLO checkpoint.** That guard is
load-bearing rather than defensive: a `pool` branch's weights are
grid-independent, so an SLO checkpoint would load with no shape error and then
be silently conditioned on a field it never saw.

## 3. Variable Time-Step ($\Delta t$) Conditioning

Unlike original uniform-grid PDEs, clinical visits have irregular intervals and no shared $t=0$.

- **Residual Formulation:** The solver acts as a proper $\Delta t$-conditioned Euler residual operator: $u_{t+\Delta t} = u_t + \Delta t \cdot f_\theta(u_t, \Delta t)$. **The $\times\Delta t$ scaling was ablated directly and is a measured null.** On the mask channel the Euler form turns out to be a pure *reparameterisation* rather than an inductive bias: the residual target $(y-u_t)/\Delta t$ at flipped pixels is exactly $\pm z_{\text{gap}}/\Delta t$ at every $\Delta t$ bucket, so $f_\theta$ must synthesise an internal $\div\Delta t$ that the outer $\times\Delta t$ cancels — $f_\theta$ is not a queryable rate field. Dropping the output multiply while keeping $\Delta t$ as an input feature (`--euler_dt_scale False`) gave $+0.0134 \pm 0.0059$ SE across the 5-fold CV (4/5 folds, $p \approx 0.09$), below the $\approx 0.016$ that the replicate floor demands of a 5-fold paired mean, so per the pre-registered rule the leg does not graduate: the canonical operator stays $u + \Delta t \cdot f_\theta$, the one MP-PDE defines (Brandstetter, Worrall & Welling, ICLR 2022), which also preserves the $\Delta t \to 0$ persistence limit. **The "the solver learns a rate field" framing is dropped as a measured 5-fold negative result** — a reportable outcome, not a defeat.
- **Time integration is not a binding constraint either.** Integrating the same vector field with a fixed-step explicit Runge–Kutta wrapper — a Neural ODE in the sense of Chen, Rubanova, Bettencourt & Duvenaud, NeurIPS 2018, with the field made autonomous by zeroing its $\Delta t$ slot — is a 5-fold null against the single Euler step: $-0.0119 \pm 0.0037$ SE (1/5 folds) for RK4 over the locked stencil backbone, and RK4 costs four to five times the per-epoch wall-clock of one Euler step on that backbone. Most of that delta is the wrapper's own replicate-scale offset rather than the integrator: on fold 2 the wrapper at euler/1 reproduces the locked operator to $-0.010$, removing the $\Delta t$ input feature outright costs a further $-0.005$, and raising the order from Euler to RK4 costs $-0.0002$ — the most exact null in the project. The same holds over a global spectral operator (RK4 over the FNO is $+0.0062 \pm 0.0056$ against plain FNO, 4/5 folds). **Accuracy alone does not license an "ODE" claim** — the endpoint metric is binarised and blind to the intermediate trajectory — **and the diagnostic that does has now run** (2026-09-17, post-hoc, no training): re-evaluating a trained checkpoint under several inference schemes and step counts, a genuine continuous model should barely move. **The locked model fails it** (Dice spans 0.0856 across euler $n \in \{1,2,4\}$ and rk4 $n \in \{1,2\}$ — 2.4× the single-fold floor — with the Euler refinements growing rather than converging), so no "ODE", "rate field" or "learned dynamics" sentence is licensed for it or for any headline result. **The arm trained under an autonomous RK4 field passes it** (four refined settings within 0.0044, below the replicate floor), so a continuous-time reading belongs to that arm alone. The pair is the finding: training under a fixed-step RK scheme does produce a genuinely solver-independent vector field, and that model is a 5-fold null on accuracy at ~4.8× the cost — the ODE property is real, obtainable, and worth nothing here.
- **Persistence as an Inductive Prior:** The leading $u_t$ term is not just the Euler base needed to make the residual form well-posed — it is a deliberate inductive prior, justified on two independent grounds. *(i) Numerical / Euler-base:* framing the network as a residual operator that predicts only the increment $\Delta t \cdot f_\theta$ on top of the known state $u_t$ is far better-conditioned than regressing the absolute next state $u_{t+\Delta t}$ from scratch — consecutive visits are almost identical, so the base term carries most of the signal and the network is freed to model only the change. *(ii) Clinical:* geographic atrophy evolves **slowly** — the lesion barely changes between visits, so persistence ($u_{t+\Delta t} \approx u_t$) is already a strong baseline — and **monotonically** — atrophic retinal tissue never regenerates, so GA is strictly growing and never shrinks. The model therefore only has to learn a small, growth-only correction on top of the persistence base rather than the entire field. A soft monotonic-mask penalty exists to enforce the same prior explicitly at training time (see [ARCHITECTURE.md §7](ARCHITECTURE.md)), but it is **locked off** (`--monotonic_mask_weight 0.0`) in every run of the hand-in batch, on two measurements: the 2026-08-15 loss ladder found it makes no difference to change-region Dice ($-0.0032 \pm 0.0033$ SE, better on only 7 of 20 paired epochs), and the F2 audit found that under pushforward its "already atrophied" reference is the model's *own* rolled prediction, so it ratchets the model's own false positives at $31.8\times$ the data gradient. The persistence base therefore carries the monotonicity assumption on its own, and the model is *not* architecturally prevented from predicting shrinkage — deliberately so, since the ground-truth segmentations do appear to shrink locally in places, and whether that is real biology or segmentation/registration noise has not yet been quantified.
- **Time Encoding Flag:** `delta_adapted` mode uses per-window $\Delta t$ (in years) attached to `data.dt`. The GNN reads `dt_per_node` as the integration step.
- **Dual-Branch Composition** (revised 2026-07-19 — see the divergence note below):
  - Main branch (uniform mesh) returns full next-state: $u + \Delta t \cdot f_\theta$.
  - Correction branch (moving mesh) returns pure delta: $\Delta t \cdot g_\theta$.
  - The interpolated moved-branch correction is scaled by a **learnable scalar gate** $\alpha$ (initialised to 0), so the moved branch starts inert and has to earn its contribution. This is the ReZero construction (Bachlechner, Majumder, Mao, Cottrell & McAuley, UAI 2021): a normally-initialised sublayer multiplied by a zero-initialised scalar. $\alpha$ also pins the moved term's scale, which the loss alone does not: the loss constrains only the *sum* of the branches, so without $\alpha$ the branches are free to grow large and mutually cancelling. The gate and the decoder's zero-init are deliberately **mutually exclusive**. Both branches would otherwise zero-initialise the decoder's final layer so that each starts at its own residual identity, but doing that on the gated branch as well pins $g_\theta \equiv 0$ *and* $\alpha = 0$ at once, so $\partial L/\partial\alpha$ and $\partial L/\partial\theta_b$ vanish together — an exact deadlock from which the correction branch can never activate. Had that shipped, "$\alpha \to 0$" would have been an initialisation artefact reported as the headline negative result. The correction branch therefore takes a normally-initialised decoder and lets $\alpha$ alone supply the zero start.
  - Summation evaluates to: $pred = u + \Delta t \cdot f_\theta + \alpha \cdot \tilde{I}\big[\Delta t \cdot g_\theta\big]$.
  - $\tilde{I}$ denotes ItpNet interpolation whose weights are **renormalised to sum to 1 wherever the raw row-sum magnitude is at least `MIN_ROWSUM` = 0.1**; smaller rows are divided by a sign-preserving 0.1 floor instead, so they keep $|\text{sum}|/0.1 < 1$ and the amplification is capped at $10\times$. At the canonical $k = 12$ the floor engages on ~12.6 % of moved→uniform rows (and on none at $k = 30$). ItpNet's last layer is linear, so the weights are unconstrained reals: this is a *normalised* combination, not a convex one — constant-preserving on the un-floored rows, with the gain bounded by the floor rather than by the normalisation.
  - At $\Delta t \to 0$ the prediction collapses to persistence $u$, which is the right limit for clinical visits with very short gaps.
  - **Removed: the residual-cut term $\mathrm{res\_cut}(u)$.** A per-timestep CNN ItpNet head compensating for moved↔uniform interpolation error, it was previously added into the *velocity* as $+\,\Delta t \cdot \mathrm{res\_cut}(u)$. Because its input is the state $u$ and the ItpNet round-trip pre-training taught it $\mathrm{res\_cut}(u) \approx u - \tilde{I}[u_{\text{moved}}]$ (the full-state round-trip error, $\approx 0.8\,u$), it is a state-aligned velocity that compounds over an autoregressive rollout, and nothing in the loss penalises its magnitude. Empirically it drove the dual-branch model to diverge (rollout RMSE $3.3\times10^5 \to 1.2\times10^{12}$ across epochs 2–4). It was first made default-off, then cut from the framework entirely — the ItpNet head, the composition term, the epoch-0 pre-training of it, and the CLI switches are all gone.
  - $\alpha$'s trajectory over training is a **reportable measurement**, and it has now been measured (2026-08-17, 5-fold CV + param-matched bypass control). **The reading originally stated here — "$\alpha \to 0$ means mesh adaptation does not transfer to slow-progressing GA" — is retracted as an inference.** Measured: $\alpha$ starts at exactly $\pm 0.002$ (= $\text{lr}\cdot\text{sign}(g)$ from the zero init, so the construction behaves), peaks at 0.058–0.093, and ends $\approx 0$ on every fold — i.e. $\alpha$ never opens. But the **bypass control**, which loads the same DMM and then ignores its warp (uniform mesh), gives the *same* trajectory: with that arm complete at 5/5 folds its peaks span 0.031–0.101, i.e. they *bracket* the dual range rather than sitting below it, and the single largest peak in either arm belongs to a bypass fold. On the prediction side the two are equally flat — dual $-$ bypass $= -0.0001 \pm 0.0060$ SE on change-region Dice, the tightest null in the project. Since the mesh plays no role in that control, $\alpha \to 0$ cannot be evidence about mesh adaptation.
  - **The mechanism is NOT established, and an earlier reading here was retracted (2026-08-24).** This bullet previously stated that the correction branch's gradient "underflows to exactly 0 on 78–85 % of steps". That is withdrawn: `clip_grad_norm_` returns exactly 0.0 when every parameter's `.grad` is `None`, so a logged zero is indistinguishable from "this module took no backward on this step", and it is not evidence of underflow. A probe on the real dual stack finds the gradient intact and **exactly proportional to $\alpha$** ($|\nabla\theta_b|$ = 0.0034 at $\alpha = 0.002$, 0.34 at $\alpha = 0.2$, 1.799 at $\alpha = 1$), so at the $\alpha$ the runs actually reach the correction branch's learning signal is roughly $60\times$ weaker than the uniform branch's — a self-reinforcing dead gate rather than a severed path. What produces the logged zeros has not been determined and should not be asserted.
  - **Three later arms fix the scope of the negative result, and none of them opens the branch.** *(i)* Removing the gate (`--use_alpha_gate False`, fold 2) is decisively worse — late change-region Dice 0.3148 against the gated arm's 0.4878, $-0.173$, roughly five times the single-fold floor — while `grad_norm/moved` stays 0 at the median: the gate is *protective* of the prediction, not inert. *(ii)* Removing the weight decay from the gated machinery (`--moved_weight_decay 0`, arm G, fold 2) is a null against its correct twin ($+0.0092$, under the 0.036 single-fold floor), with $\alpha$ peaking *lower* decay-free (0.058 vs 0.067) and the moved branch's parameter norm *growing*; $\alpha$ itself has had zero weight decay since 2026-07-19, before every recorded dual run. *(iii)* On the v2 dilated-stencil backbone the dual arm still trails its own single twin by $-0.031$ (fold 2). **The honest negative result is therefore "the gated correction branch does not engage — mesh or no mesh, decay or no decay, gate or no gate"** — an implementation-scoped finding, not a claim about GA. Scope note: the 5-fold dual and bypass arms were trained on the v1 k-NN + mean+max backbone, not the locked stencil of §5.
  - **Gradients are clipped per module, not jointly** (`--clip_mode per_module` at `--grad_clip 1.0`, the locked default), over five disjoint groups: the uniform branch, the moved branch, ItpNet's weights, $\alpha$, and the LayerEncoder — the DMM is frozen and excluded. A single joint clip scales every parameter by one shared coefficient, so a spike anywhere throttles the whole model, and in the dual arm the spikes are not rare: ItpNet's pre-clip gradient runs at a median of $10^3$–$10^4$ on the k-NN-era dual runs against the uniform branch's $\approx 1$. Under one joint clip the healthy uniform branch would have had its updates divided by that, which is why **every dual-vs-single comparison made before the per-module split (2026-08-08) compared a throttled model against an unthrottled one** rather than two architectures. $\alpha$ is its own group for the same reason in miniature: grouped with ItpNet's weights its gradient would be rescaled by their norm, an optimiser-mechanical push toward "$\alpha$ stays 0" in precisely the quantity being measured. Caveat for the write-up: joint clipping is still implemented and bit-reproducible (`--clip_mode global`), but it has **never been run as a control** — no row in `solver_results.csv` carries that value; the 35 rows that carry none at all are the pre-flag runs that clipped jointly because there was no alternative. The contrast above therefore rests on re-reading those runs' logged norms, not on a matched same-era pair.
  - Cost: the dual branch runs at order $10$–$11\times$ the wall-clock of its single twin — $10.7\times$ on the least-contended folds (1026 vs 96 s/epoch), $11.1\times$ on the fold medians (1144 vs 103) — for a Dice delta inside the replicate floor (dual $-$ single $= -0.0013 \pm 0.0086$ SE at 5 folds). Quote that ratio to one significant figure only: per-epoch times are contaminated by GPU co-scheduling [mechanism refuted 2026-09-17], so the minimum across folds is the least-contended observation. See [SOLVER_FINAL_RUNS.md](GraphPDE/demos & explanations/SOLVER_FINAL_RUNS.md) §5.

## 4. Surrogate Equation Encoder (LayerEncoder)

To provide the GNN with a "surrogate equation identity" (a stand-in for PDE coefficients $\theta_{PDE}$), a lightweight `LayerEncoder` CNN compresses the 10 retinal layer boundaries into a compact global embedding vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{embed}}$.

- **Status: off in the locked recipe and in every headline arm** (`--d_embed 0`; the encoder is only constructed when `d_embed > 0`). The only runs that enable it are the 20 T5 ablation runs below. The T5 ablation swept $d_{embed} \in \{32, 64, 128, 256\}$ at 5 folds and is a null at every width — all four within $\pm 0.004$ of the encoder-off arm, no monotonic trend, and mildly negative per eye ($d_{embed} = 256$: $-0.0087 \pm 0.0069$, 30/75 eyes) — at roughly 30–55 % more compute. The surrogate-encoder contribution is therefore reported as a **measured negative result**, not as an active component of the model. The description below is what the component does when it is switched on.
- **Rectangular Handling:** the first downsampling block halves the long axis only (`stride=(1,2)`) and the two later blocks halve both, so the stack reduces rows by 4 and columns by 8. That is a $2\times$ correction against the ~21:1 physical anisotropy, not a fix for it: the final receptive field is still ~14$\times$ skewed (11 rows $\approx$ 1.36 mm against 17 columns $\approx$ 0.097 mm). `AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1)` keeps the encoder spatial-size agnostic.
- **Integration:** This global per-graph embedding is broadcast per-node and concatenated to the `variables` input of the GNN alongside $\Delta t$ and patient covariates, telling the dynamics model what kind of retinal structure it is operating on without requiring the GNN to parse raw layer geometry through message passing.

## 5. The graph-solver backbone: MP-PDE & MM-PDE conceptual modifications

**This is one of the two backbone sections — read it with §5b.** It covers the
architecture the project started from and the changes the clinical setting
forced on it; §5b covers the other eight arms the same framework runs, on the
same terms. Several decisions recorded here (the $K=1$ rule, the decoder, the
graph construction) are *framework-level* rather than GNN-level and are noted as
such where they bind the other arms too.

The two frameworks this project adapts are **MP-PDE** (Brandstetter, Worrall &
Welling, "Message Passing Neural PDE Solvers", ICLR 2022) and **MM-PDE** (Hu,
Wang & Ma, "Better Neural PDE Solvers Through Data-Free Mesh Movers", ICLR
2024), the latter supplying the data-free mesh mover and its Monge–Ampère
objective used in §2.

- **Temporal Bundling Removed ($K=1$):** Temporal bundling (used in the original MP-PDE) is redundant due to the short sequences inherent to clinical data. More critically, MM-PDE does not natively support multi-timestep windows because the DMM computes the moved mesh from a single spatial snapshot. If $K>1$ were used, the mesh generated at $t=0$ would become "stale" for later timesteps in the window. The framework therefore defaults to strict $K=1$ autoregressive prediction to ensure the mesh faithfully reflects the current state.
- **Output decoder replaced (2026-07-05):** upstream MP-PDE decodes the processed hidden state with a 1-D convolution *along the hidden axis*, whose purpose there is to emit the $K$ bundled future frames. At this project's $K = 1$ that head degenerates. The convolution stack bottoms out at length 1, so all $C \cdot K = 11$ output channels become an affine function of a single scalar per node — a rank-1 bottleneck that cannot represent independent per-channel dynamics, which is a poor fit for a state whose binary mask and ten layer-depth channels evolve differently — and the same arithmetic forced `hidden_dim` $\ge 113$ for the head to build at all, which would have blocked the small models the capacity ablations later settled on. It is replaced by a full-rank three-layer MLP mapping the hidden state directly to the $C \cdot K$ residual, with a bare final `Linear` because the output is an unbounded signed residual. This is a deliberate departure from the upstream architecture rather than a faithful port of it, and it invalidated every checkpoint written before the change.
- **Uniform-graph construction and neighbour aggregation (decided 2026-09-03):** the 2026-05-23 index-space k-NN restored cross-row connectivity on the 49×1024 grid but left the neighbour *placement* ~21× anisotropic in physical space — at k=12 a node reaches ±0.25 mm across B-scans but only ±0.011 mm along them per hop, against a GA front that advances a median ~12 columns (≈0.07 mm; q3 17, p90 23) per 180-day visit. Neither depth nor a larger index-space k buys that reach (both measured null). The canonical uniform graph is therefore a **dilated stencil**: rows ±1 × columns {0, ±7, ±14, ±21}, 20 neighbours, ±0.12 mm on both axes — built at forward time by a thin wrapper around the unchanged message-passing GNN (`GraphPDE/baselines/anisognn.py`, `--backbone anisognn`; parameters unchanged), with **mean-only** neighbour aggregation (`--gnn_aggr mean`, 67,147 parameters). Measured on the same network, loss, data and seed: **+0.074 ± 0.008 change-region Dice over the k-NN GNN at 5 folds (5/5 folds, 74/75 eyes)** — the largest effect in the project, larger than the U-Net's own margin — leaving the GNN statistically indistinguishable from the parameter-matched U-Net (Ronneberger, Fischer & Brox, MICCAI 2015); a **5-fold** reach ladder places a knee at ±21 columns (≈ the p90 front advance), degrading on both sides — ±12 columns −0.019 ± 0.008 (1/5 folds) and ±42 columns −0.026 ± 0.004 (0/5), both over the paired floor — with the other two geometries also below the default at 5 folds but on opposite sides of the floor: ±35 columns at unchanged density −0.010 ± 0.003 (1/5, under the paired floor, which also resolves the ±42-column density confound) and rows ±2 −0.039 ± 0.005 (0/5), the largest geometry penalty measured; mean-only equals mean+max on both graphs at 0.89× the parameters, and **k=20 in index space (the same reach as k=12) buys nothing at 5 folds** — −0.010 ± 0.005 against the matched mean-aggregation k=12 arm (1/5 folds; +0.000 ± 0.005 against the mean+max arm, 3/5) and −0.001 per eye (36/75). Placement, not count. (An earlier fold-2 screen read +0.022 for k=20 and was quoted as "a quarter of the gain"; it sat under the 0.036 single-fold floor and did not survive promotion to 5 folds.) The moved branch is unaffected: its nodes leave the lattice on the DMM-warped mesh, so it keeps its own k-NN — built, since 2026-08-06, in isotropic **index** space (`--iso_edges True`) rather than in physical mm, which on this grid returned 100 % same-row neighbours and invalidated every dual-branch result before that date. **Gate discharged 2026-09-03:** the pre-committed seed-7 replicate reproduces the lift (+0.0736 ± 0.0162 SE, 5/5 folds; seed-pooled 144/150 eyes), so the lock stands; the k-NN + mean+max configuration remains the reported comparison arm, and `edges_global.pt` remains the data graph *that arm* consumes — not the moved branch's, which rebuilds its own as described above. Implication for the thesis: the GNN's earlier deficit against the U-Net was its graph, not its operator class, capacity, normalisation or direction features.
- **Normalisation is per-node `LayerNorm`, and that is a load-bearing choice rather than a default** (`--norm_type layer`). The pushforward curriculum unrolls its intermediate steps under `no_grad` but in `train()` mode, so a BatchNorm placed there would renormalise the deliberately drifted state — erasing the very perturbation the pushforward exists to teach against — while writing those drifted statistics into the running estimates that evaluation later uses. A normalisation that keeps no running statistics makes the operator identical in `train()` and `eval()` and removes both problems at once; it must also be strictly per-sample, which PyTorch Geometric's own `LayerNorm` is *not* at its default setting (it normalises a whole batch as one graph and would reinstate the cross-sample coupling being removed). `batch` survives only as an ablation and no reported run uses it. **The competing hypothesis — that the U-Net's advantage (§6) is its normalisation carrying a spatially *global* statistic, which a message-passing GNN could have too — was implemented and measured, and is not sufficient.** `--norm_type graph` gives the GNN exactly that: a per-graph mean and variance over all nodes and features, the graph analogue of the U-Net's `GroupNorm(1, C)`, at identical parameter cost. Across four arms it never graduates. On the k-NN GNN it reads $+0.0146 \pm 0.0074$ SE fold-paired (4/5 folds) and $+0.0129 \pm 0.0060$ per eye (42/75), graduating on neither instrument; combined with mean-only aggregation it reads $+0.0196 \pm 0.0088$ at seed 42 but only $+0.0058 \pm 0.0059$ at seed 7, so it does not replicate; matching the U-Net's *density* of normalised nonlinearities as well (a norm between every `Linear` and `ReLU`) adds nothing on top of it ($+0.0081 \pm 0.0087$, 3/5); and on the locked stencil backbone it is a null ($-0.0121$ at fold 2, under the 0.036 single-fold floor). Together those close the normalisation axis: a global normalisation statistic is not what the U-Net's advantage is made of, and `layer` stays canonical.
- **Training Separation:** ItpNet is pre-trained with its own optimizer so that it does not pollute the main optimiser or scheduler before real-data training begins. Its objective is the uniform → moved → uniform **round-trip identity**, scored by the channel-weighted MSE *alone* — deliberately without the soft-Dice term of the main loss, because at the exact identity the MSE term is zero while soft-Dice is not, so including it would give the interpolator a non-zero gradient at the very configuration it is being taught to reproduce and push it to over-sharpen. The pre-training is a fixed number of loader passes at epoch 0 of the main run (locked at 25) rather than a separate stage.
- **Pushforward training, re-denominated in elapsed time.** The pushforward trick itself is inherited from MP-PDE: some training steps start not from an observed state but from the model's own earlier prediction (the chain is unrolled under `no_grad`, with the gradient taken only on the final step), so the operator is trained on the perturbed inputs it will actually meet during an autoregressive rollout instead of only on clean ones. What is this project's own is the **curriculum**: upstream counts unroll depth in timesteps, which is not a comparable quantity when visit intervals differ between eyes, so depth here is a *budget in elapsed days*. Each pass draws a budget from $\{0, B, 2B, \ldots\}$ with $B = 180$ days — the cohort's **modal** visit interval — sampling is restricted to windows that can actually reach that budget, and each eye then chains as many of its own visits as fit without overshooting it. Two eyes on different visit schedules therefore train to the same elapsed horizon, which is what makes them comparable under a time-anchored evaluation. The choice of base is not cosmetic: an earlier hard-coded 90-day quantum was the cohort's *smallest* gap rather than its modal one, and because the chain undershoots rather than overshoots, a 90-day budget could never link two visits at all — measured 0 of 75 eyes. The locked recipe runs `--unrolling 2`, a 360-day maximum horizon chosen to match the @360d evaluation anchor, where 74 of 75 eyes reach at least one unrolled step.
- **Loss Weighting (the locked objective):** the base term is a channel-weighted MSE emphasising the clinical deliverable — weight 5.0 on the mask channel against 1.0 on each of the ten auxiliary layer channels. Two auxiliary terms sit on top, and the 2026-08-15 loss ladder settled both. Plain MSE on the mask is dominated by the ~99 % of pixels that never change between visits, and the earliest single-branch runs collapsed to bit-exact copy-input: every validation eye's thresholded prediction at 360 d equalled its own baseline mask. The ladder localised the cure precisely — **it is the channel weighting, not the soft-Dice term, that escapes the collapse** (mask×5 with no Dice matches the full objective within noise, $-0.008 \pm 0.015$ SE paired per eye), while raw unweighted MSE escapes only around epoch 26 and never recovers (late mean 0.032). The escape epoch is a clean dose–response in mask-channel gradient strength: epoch 0 at sharpness 2, 2 at sharpness 4, 5 at sharpness 6, 9 at mask×5 alone, 26 at mask×1. A **soft-Dice term on the mask channel** (weight 5.0, sharpness 2.0; the differentiable Dice objective of Milletari, Navab & Ahmadi, 3DV 2016) is therefore **kept for stability rather than for accuracy**: it restores gradient in the binarisation band at the moving boundary, buying immediate escape and roughly halving the epoch-to-epoch variance, at no gain in final score. The **soft monotonic-mask penalty** is **locked off** (weight 0.0, §3). Both auxiliary terms are scoped **per graph** rather than pooled over the batch — each is a ratio, so a pooled denominator made one eye's gradient depend on which eyes it happened to be batched with, and disagreed with the per-eye-then-averaged evaluation. One accepted side effect: soft-Dice's shallow sigmoid does not saturate at the physical values, so it decalibrates the raw mask regression (~1.6× stretch about the threshold, roughly a third of the reported mask rollout MSE). It cannot flip a binarised metric, but any table quoting raw mask RMSE needs the caveat.
- **Intermediate-Time-Point Regularization (evaluated and removed 2026-06-11):** Clinical visits give only sparse observations of a continuous trajectory, so a $\Delta t$-conditioned operator easily overfits the endpoints while behaving arbitrarily in between. To address this, a regulariser was implemented (2026-05-21, supervisor's recipe) that queried the model at $K$ sub-times $\tau_k = \alpha_k\,\Delta t$ with $\alpha_k\sim U(0.1,0.9)$ per graph and penalised the prediction against the linear interpolant $(1-\alpha_k)\,u_t + \alpha_k\,u_{t+\Delta t}$ under the same data criterion. Ablations (REG-weight sweep λ ∈ {1, 3, 10} plus reg-on/off legs of the 2026-05-25 queues) showed no benefit on the change-region Dice metric — the reg=0 configurations matched or beat the regularised ones — so the feature was removed from the codebase on 2026-06-11. Historical regularised runs remain reproducible from their recorded git SHAs; the negative result is reportable in the thesis Discussion.

## 5b. The backbone family: what the framework is agnostic to

**The peer of §5.** Once the framework of §0 is fixed, $f_\theta$ is a free
slot, and filling it with architectures from unrelated literatures is how the
"what components are needed" question is actually answered. These arms are
*not* a validation exercise bolted onto a graph-solver thesis — one of them
(the dilated-stencil wrapper) **is** the locked model, another (the T-FEN)
posts the highest mean recorded, and the project's central architectural claim
rests on the contrast between three of them. Code lives in
`GraphPDE/baselines/`, one module per architecture, reached through a lazy
`--backbone` dispatch; run outputs land in the normal results tree and are
harvested by the same collector.

**The contract, and why it is what makes the survey a measurement.** A backbone
module contains *architecture only*. It is constructed from the same meta
($K = 1$, $C = 11$, the covariate count), consumes the same PyG batch, returns
the full next state, emits `u + Δt·f` with a **zero-initialised head so it
starts at exact persistence**, uses **no normalisation carrying batch
statistics** (the pushforward unrolls under `no_grad` in `train()` mode, so
running statistics would make training and evaluation different operators), and
exposes `vector_field` — the rate before the Euler composition — computed from
the same body as `forward`, so the two cannot drift. Loss, curriculum,
optimiser, budget, evaluation, selection and seeds all come from the shared
pipeline, and a backbone is forbidden to carry training knobs of its own. The
alternative — one pipeline per architecture — would un-control the comparison
and recreate this repository's most documented defect class, two copies of one
computation drifting apart. The dense arms additionally receive *byte-identical*
conditioning (Δt, the two covariates and two coordinate ramps as input planes),
which is what licenses reading an FNO-vs-U-Net difference as an operator
contrast rather than a conditioning one.

**The arms, and what each was built to isolate.**

- **Dilated-stencil GNN** (`anisognn`, 67,147 params) — the unchanged
  message-passing network with its k-NN `edge_index` swapped at forward time
  for rows ±1 × columns {0, ±7, ±14, ±21}. Isolates *graph geometry* from
  operator class, capacity and every other GNN knob, and is the **locked
  model** since 2026-09-03 (§5).
- **U-Net**, parameter-matched at 83,081 (Ronneberger, Fischer & Brox, MICCAI
  2015) — the standard strong surrogate in the PDE-surrogate literature and,
  independently, the GA-domain precedent (Salvi et al., *Ophthalmology Science*
  5(2):100635, 2025). It is the arm that first showed the original graph
  construction was the problem. Its **44× capacity control** (3.35 M params)
  exists to answer the obvious reviewer objection and answers it against the
  U-Net: the big one *loses*.
- **FNO** (79,160 params; Li et al., ICLR 2021) — global spectral support with
  *no* local path, the clean test of whether context alone is the ingredient.
  It is not: it lands between the GNN and the U-Net and is established below
  the U-Net at the one-year anchor, while winning the long-horizon bins.
- **Localized-kernel FNO** (79,960 params; Liu-Schiaffini et al., ICML 2024) —
  the same operator plus a 3×3 per-block bypass, i.e. FNO + the one missing
  ingredient, +800 parameters. It ties the U-Net. With the FNO arm this is the
  measurement behind "context and local detail are separable and both are
  required", and it is the one claim here supported by two architecturally
  unrelated constructions landing in the same place.
- **Graph U-Net** (72,713 params) — the U-Net's pyramid with every convolution
  replaced by one message-passing layer. Isolates the *pyramid* at a fixed
  operator class and the *operator class* at a fixed pyramid; it sits between
  the two, which is the expected reading and worth reporting as such.
- **Finite Element Network / T-FEN** (68,503 params; Lienen & Günnemann, ICLR
  2022) — P1 elements on the lattice's triangulation, lumped mass, the paper's
  free-form term, plus a learned per-cell **advection velocity**. The explicit
  transport term is the only *mechanism* in the survey that graduates on its
  own (+0.053 ± 0.007, 5/5 folds, both seeds) and it recovers the near-term
  growth bin to the dilated stencil's own value — the reach result reproduced
  by a different mechanism. **Two caveats are not optional.** *(a) The
  transport comparison is the one architecture claim here that is NOT
  parameter-matched.* The control is the same network with the velocity head
  deleted — structurally the cleanest kind of ablation, purely additive, both
  heads zero-init so the two arms start as the same function — but that head is
  **33,718 parameters on top of a 34,785-parameter free-form term**, so the
  comparison is 68,503 against 34,785, a factor of **1.97×**. A
  parameter-matched free-form control (width ≈ 139) has **never been run**.
  What argues against reading +0.053 as capacity is indirect: capacity is a
  measured null on every other axis in this project (the 44× U-Net loses to the
  matched one, GNN depth/width/degree are flat, the surrogate encoder is null
  across an 8× width range), and the effect is mechanism-specific — ~4–5 % of
  the rate magnitude, concentrated in the near-term bin — rather than a diffuse
  capacity gain. State the gap; do not claim the comparison controls for it.
  *(b) The arm as trained is not a valid long-horizon integrator* (its free-running rollout
  diverges on 5 of 10 runs and 9/10 final checkpoints leave the explicit
  scheme's stability region on at least one advected channel), so the
  **learned velocity map and every raw field must not be quoted** — the
  binarised anchors are unaffected and stand. Halving the step does not fix it
  (the CFL bound is necessary, not sufficient), and the principled remedy, a
  velocity head bounded in its parameterisation, is *deliberately parked*: the
  instability is reported as the finding.
- **Fixed-step Runge–Kutta integration** (`--integrator`, no parameters; Chen
  et al., NeurIPS 2018) — a wrapper that integrates any backbone's
  `vector_field` over each graph's own Δt, with the field made autonomous by
  zeroing its Δt slot. It is a null on accuracy over both a local graph
  operator and a global spectral one (§3). Its separate value is diagnostic:
  the arm *trained* under autonomous RK4 is the only checkpoint in the project
  that passes the solver-swap test (its Dice moves by 0.0044 across inference
  schemes, below the replicate floor), while the locked model fails it
  (spread 0.0856, with Euler refinements diverging). So a continuous-time
  reading is licensed for that arm alone — and that arm is a null on accuracy,
  which is itself the result: on this task the ODE property is obtainable and
  buys nothing.
- **Per-pixel floor** (`--hidden_layers 0`, 14,795 params) — the GNN's own
  class with message passing switched off. Not a baseline in the usual sense
  but a *calibration*: it reads exactly 0.0000 on all five folds, bit-identical
  to persistence, which is what licenses reading every point of change-region
  Dice as coming from spatial context.

**Deliberately not run,** so the survey's boundary is explicit: PINNs (the
governing equation is unknown, the residual would be taken on a binary mask,
and the method is per-instance rather than amortised); a transformer backbone
and a plain RNN (the two remaining rungs of the locality ladder); and any
architecture needing a dependency outside torch.

## 6. Evaluation Metrics & Baselines

- **Primary Clinical Metrics:** Dice and IoU on the denormalized, thresholded Mask (Channel 0) at 360 days. Focus on the *change region* — `change_region_dice_360d`, the Dice between the two symmetric differences against the baseline mask, $\{i : \hat u_i \neq u^{0}_i\}$ and $\{i : u^{T}_i \neq u^{0}_i\}$, on the binarised mask channel. It is deliberately **symmetric rather than growth-only**, so a predicted *shrinkage* counts as a predicted change and is scored against the truth — consistent with §3, where the model is not architecturally prevented from predicting shrinkage. This is *not* the Mai growth-region Dice reported per time bin, which is the asymmetric followup $\cap$ NOT-baseline and is computed separately in `_compute_step_metrics_mai`; both have a persistence floor of 0, so the two are easy to conflate. Reported as a **late-epoch mean ± sd** (epochs $\ge$ 10), never as a best-epoch value, which at this variance is noise. Two caveats belong in the thesis text: (i) the endpoint fires at the *first* rollout step whose cumulative time reaches the horizon and has **no upper bound**, so 68/75 eyes are scored at exactly 360 d but **7/75 (9.3 %) at 450–720 d**, where the ground-truth change region is 1.3–1.7× larger — accepted deliberately for comparability with Mai et al., *Ophthalmology Science* 4:100466 (2024); (ii) pad-aware variants are reported alongside (a pad-masked `_anat` column and a border-touching vs interior split) because the fixed crop censors lesion growth at the window edge (§1). **Every delta must be read against the replicate floor:** same-configuration re-runs differ with sd $\approx 0.013$ ($n = 7$ pairs), so a single-fold delta needs $> 0.036$ and a 5-fold paired mean $> 0.016$ before it counts as an effect. Notably the same-seed spread exceeds the cross-seed spread, i.e. GPU nondeterminism dominates seed choice.
- **Secondary Metrics:** Rollout MSE/RMSE (Note: Per-pixel MSE is flawed for slow-moving GA due to the persistence baseline).
- **Baselines and the arm table.** Three internal controls plus the full backbone survey of §5b; every arm below is 5-fold at the locked loss and reported as a late-epoch mean ± sd, so all deltas are read against the 0.016 paired / 0.036 single-fold floor. **The whole family on one instrument**, ranked (late-epoch `change_region_dice_360d`, seed 42, against a persistence floor of 0 by construction; cost is the minimum epoch time across folds, order-of-magnitude only):

The **single-branch solver** (`--moving_mesh False`), which since 2026-09-03 means the v2 dilated-stencil GNN (0.5258 ± 0.0497), with the k=12 index-space k-NN + mean+max GNN (0.4515 ± 0.0429) kept as the reported comparison arm rather than as the baseline. The **parameter-matched bypass control** (`--bypass_dmm_move`), which loads the DMM, runs the second GNN and the full ItpNet round-trip but replaces the moved mesh with the uniform grid. The bypass arm is load-bearing rather than decorative: it separates "the mesh *geometry* contributes" from "the extra trainable parameters contribute" — that second branch plus ItpNet is **~100 k trainable parameters** (175,663 vs 75,339 on the k-NN arms, 159,279 vs 67,147 on the stencil arms), not the ~1 M an earlier version of this bullet claimed; the DMM's own 1.39 M parameters are frozen, excluded from the optimiser, and loaded by the bypass arm too, so they are not part of the capacity being controlled for. It is also the control that forced the retraction recorded in §3.
  - *The backbone survey (§5b; historically "Stage F5" in the run log).* A **per-pixel floor** (`--hidden_layers 0`) reads exactly 0.0000 on all five folds — bit-identical to persistence, so every point of change-region Dice any model earns comes from spatial context. The **parameter-matched U-Net** (Ronneberger, Fischer & Brox, MICCAI 2015; chosen also because a 2-D U-Net is the GA-domain precedent — Salvi et al., *Ophthalmology Science* 5(2):100635, 2025) reads 0.5046 ± 0.0632, i.e. +0.0531 ± 0.0226 over the k-NN GNN at 4/5 folds, and replicates at a second seed (0.5199, 5/5 folds); its **capacity control** — the same U-Net at 3.35 M parameters, 40× the param-matched version and 44× the GNN — reads 0.4566 ± 0.0526 and *loses* to the small one by −0.048 ± 0.030 (1/5 folds), so the U-Net's win is an architecture-class effect and not a parameter-count effect. **FNO** (Li, Kovachki, Azizzadenesheli, Liu, Bhattacharya, Stuart & Anandkumar, ICLR 2021) reads 0.4831 ± 0.0477 — between the two — and the **localized-kernel hybrid** that adds a 3×3 per-block bypass (Liu-Schiaffini, Berner, Bonev, Kurth, Azizzadenesheli & Anandkumar, ICML 2024) lifts it to 0.4984 ± 0.0398: global spectral support alone does not reproduce the U-Net's win, but global-plus-local does. A **graph U-Net** (0.4784 ± 0.0478) sits between the local GNN and the CNN. A **Finite Element Network** with the transport term (Lienen & Günnemann, ICLR 2022; 68,503 parameters at width 96, $1.02\times$ the locked model's 67,147) reads 0.5337 ± 0.0453, the highest 5-fold mean recorded, and its transport term is established at +0.0527 ± 0.0065 over the free-form-only control at 5/5 folds — **with the two caveats of §5b, both of which must be quoted with it**: that control is the same network with the velocity head deleted and therefore has **34,785 parameters against the T-FEN's 68,503** (a 1.97× gap, with no matched-capacity control run), and the arm **is not numerically stable as trained**: the free-running rollout diverges on 5 of 10 runs and the learned velocity exceeds the explicit scheme's stability bound on 9/10 final checkpoints, so the anchor numbers stand while the velocity field itself must not be quoted. The **Neural-ODE RK4 wrapper** (§3) is a null over both the stencil GNN and the FNO.
  - Against the locked stencil GNN the two strongest countermodels are **indistinguishable at the available precision** — neither beaten nor beating. Stencil $-$ U-Net is $+0.021 \pm 0.017$ at seed 42 and only $+0.008 \pm 0.008$ at seed 7; T-FEN $-$ stencil is $+0.008 \pm 0.008$ at seed 42 and a seed-pooled $+0.005$. The U-Net's own seed spread is about twice the GNN's, which puts a mixed paired floor near 0.020 under all of these, so none of the three orderings is quotable. The defensible reading is that the $\Delta t$-conditioned autoregressive framework transfers across architecture classes, and that the original GNN's deficit was located in its graph (§5) rather than in its operator class.

## 7. Known Issues & Future Work

- **Monitor Function Masking (Resolved 2026-05-03):** The binary GA mask produces step-function gradients ($\sim 1000\times$ larger than the smooth retinal-layer channels), which drove the Frobenius-norm monitor to $\sim 1800$ and made the second-derivative chain through $\phi$ accumulate to NaN within a few training iterations. A Gaussian blur is now applied to Channel 0 strictly for monitor function computation; the GNN itself still sees the unblurred mask. The blur is configurable via `--monitor_mask_sigma` (default 0 = off, kept so the unblurred case stays available as an ablation — and it was run: $\sigma = 0$ collapses the mesh to a near-uniform translation, so the blur is load-bearing, not cosmetic). Empirically reduces the maximum mask-*gradient* magnitude by $\sim 5\times$ ($1023 \to 204$ at $\sigma = 2$ on a synthetic step), which is what keeps the monitor finite. **The canonical $\sigma$ moved with the grid:** 4 px on the historical 256² SLO grid, and **1.0 px on the native $(49,1024)$ grid** used by the canonical checkpoint, where the sharper blur is coupled to the deepened potential head (§2).
- **Multi-Timestep DMM Integration (Future Work):** To overcome the $K=1$ limitation, future research could explore temporally aggregating the monitor function snapshot (e.g., mean or max gradient across the window) to compute a single compromise mesh. Alternatively, a hybrid architecture could be explored where the uniform branch processes all $K$ timesteps simultaneously while the moved branch runs $K$ internal autoregressive steps.
