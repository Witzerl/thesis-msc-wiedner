\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol}

This chapter answers the second part of the research question: what kind
of operator the framework of Chapter~\ref{ch:method} needs. Many operators
are run through the same slot under identical conditions. This section
defines how they are scored and how two scores are compared.

\subsection{Why Not Per-Pixel Error}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol:mse}

GA progresses slowly. Between two consecutive visits, almost all pixels of
the lesion mask stay unchanged. Persistence, which predicts no change at
all, is therefore a strong per-pixel baseline. A per-pixel mean squared
error or root mean squared error (RMSE) is dominated by the static pixels,
and a model can reach a low per-pixel error while predicting no
progression at all.

The same holds for a Dice score over the whole lesion. The static interior
of the lesion dominates it, so persistence already reaches a full-mask Dice
of about 0.87. Full-mask Dice is therefore almost blind to progression.

Raw errors are also not compared across architectures. The clinical
metrics are computed on the thresholded mask. The dense operators, such as
the U-Net and the Fourier Neural Operator, decalibrate their raw mask
regression under autoregressive rollout much more than the graph network
does: the late rollout mask RMSE of the U-Net is roughly twice that of the
graph network, while its thresholded metrics are higher. A raw-error
comparison across architecture classes would therefore measure calibration,
not prediction. The soft-Dice loss adds a separate, known stretch to the
raw mask values (\S\ref{sec:method:training:loss}). RMSE values are
computed and logged, but no RMSE value is used as evidence in this chapter.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol:metrics}

The headline metric is the change-region Dice at the one-year anchor. All
predicted and ground-truth masks are binarised at the physical threshold of
0.5, mapped into normalised space in the same way as in the loss. Let $b$
be the thresholded mask at the baseline visit, the first visit of the
rollout, $p$ the thresholded prediction at the anchor and $y$ the
thresholded ground truth at the anchor. The predicted and the true change
regions are
\begin{equation}
  \hat{C} = \{\, i : p_i \neq b_i \,\}, \qquad
  C = \{\, i : y_i \neq b_i \,\},
  \label{eq:experiments:change-region}
\end{equation}
and the change-region Dice is $2\,|\hat{C} \cap C| \,/\, (|\hat{C}| + |C|)$.

Three consequences follow. First, persistence predicts no change, so its
score is 0 by construction, and every positive value is lift over "no
progression". Second, the metric is symmetric. A predicted shrinkage counts
as predicted change and is scored against the truth, which is consistent
with the absence of a monotonic-growth constraint
(\S\ref{sec:method:dt-residual}). Third, the metric differs from the
growth-region Dice of \citet{Mai2024} defined below. Both have a
persistence floor of 0 and are easy to conflate. The change-region Dice is
also the model-selection metric. The change-region intersection over union
(IoU) is computed alongside it; full-mask Dice and IoU are reported for
comparability only.

Two variants handle the crop (\S\ref{sec:data:spatial}). The first splits
the per-eye change-region Dice by whether the ground-truth lesion at the
anchor touches the boundary of the imaged field. This boundary is
pad-aware: it is the outermost ring of the crop or any position next to the
zero-pad band, because for visits whose native grid is smaller than the
crop, the true edge of the field lies inside the crop. The interior group
is the part of the cohort whose growth is not censored by the crop. On the
canonical fold (fold 2), 6 of the 16 validation eyes are border-touching
and 10 are interior. The second variant is the same change-region Dice with
the padded grid positions excluded from both sides.

The growth-region Dice scores only new atrophy: the region
$\{\, i : y_i = 1 \text{ and } b_i = 0 \,\}$ against its predicted
counterpart. It is used by the per-eye statistical instrument
(\S\ref{sec:experiments:protocol:stats}) and in the comparison
of~\S\ref{sec:experiments:qualitative}. For that comparison, a set of
cohort metrics is also computed. Per-step records are binned by horizon
into $(0, 1]$, $(1, 2]$, $(2, 3]$ and more than three years, each with
total-mask and growth-region Dice. Lesion area is recorded in square
millimetres, together with its square root in millimetres; the
square-root transform of \citet{Feuer2013} removes the dependence of the
growth rate on the baseline lesion size. From these follow the mean
absolute error of the square-root area, per-eye growth rates (change in
square-root area per year) with the cohort Pearson correlation between
predicted and true rates, and the area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve for identifying fast progressors, defined as the top
10, 15 and 20\,\% of growth rates. \citet{Mai2024} study the same cohort
and target. Their numbers are an anchor, not a head-to-head comparison:
this thesis starts from pre-segmented masks and layer depths, whereas
\citet{Mai2024} work from raw OCT. Finally, a collapse counter records how
many eyes have a thresholded prediction at the anchor that is identical,
pixel for pixel, to their baseline mask.

\subsection{Rollout and Reporting}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol:rollout}

Two evaluation procedures run after every epoch on the validation eyes of
the fold. The one-step evaluation predicts every validation window once
from its observed input. It isolates the accuracy of a single step from the
drift of a rollout. The rollout evaluation is the procedure every reported
number comes from. For each validation eye, it starts at the baseline
visit and predicts the next visit. The prediction is then fed back as the
input of the next window, and the rollout continues over the eye's real
visit schedule, with the actual $\Delta t$ of each window.

The one-year anchor is taken at the first step whose cumulative time
reaches it (\S\ref{sec:data:temporal}). For a few eyes this step lies
beyond one year, where the true change region is larger; this is accepted
for comparability with \citet{Mai2024}. Persistence is computed inside every
rollout by the same code; for the anchor metrics it is the baseline mask
held constant. Per-step metrics are averaged per eye first and then over
the eyes, so every eye counts once, whatever its number of visits.

The checkpoint kept as the best one is the epoch with the highest
change-region Dice at the anchor. The reported numbers, however, are not
best-epoch values. With 12 to 20 validation eyes per fold, the metric
fluctuates from epoch to epoch with a standard deviation of 0.02 to 0.10,
so a best-epoch value would mostly reflect noise. Every reported number is
instead a late-epoch mean: the mean over epochs 10 to 29 of one run.

Runs are compared only when they were trained with the same version of the
pipeline. Several fixes during development changed what a run computes,
and results from before such a fix are not comparable with results after
it. Every number in this chapter comes from the final version of the
pipeline, so all of them are comparable with one another. All experiments
use the patient-level five-fold cross-validation of~\S\ref{sec:data:splits},
which has no test split; every result is a validation result.

\subsection{Two Statistical Instruments and the Noise Floor}
\label{sec:experiments:protocol:stats}

Repeated runs of the same configuration do not give identical results on
the GPU (\S\ref{sec:method:implementation}). Seven pairs of runs with the
same configuration on the same fold, two with the same seed and five with
different seeds, give a per-run standard deviation of about 0.0127 for the
late-epoch mean of the change-region Dice. Two thresholds follow from it. A
difference measured on a single fold must exceed 0.036 to count as an
effect, and a difference of five-fold paired means must exceed 0.016.
Anything smaller is treated as noise. Same-seed and different-seed repeats
spread about equally, so the seed adds nothing measurable on top of the
run-to-run variation.

This floor was measured on graph-network runs. The seed pair of the
parameter-matched U-Net spreads about 1.9 times wider. Any comparison with a
U-Net on one side is therefore read against a mixed paired floor of about
0.020. Noise affects nulls and effects differently: it can only widen a
null.

Throughout the chapter, "mean $\pm$ sd" denotes the mean and the standard
deviation over the five fold values, where each fold value is the
late-epoch mean of one run. Differences between two arms are measured with
two instruments. The first is the fold-paired mean, written
$\Delta = x \pm y$~SE ($k$/5): the mean over the five folds of the
per-fold difference, with its between-fold standard error, where $k$ is
the number of folds on which the difference has the same sign as the mean.
It is judged against the paired floor of 0.016, or 0.020 when a U-Net is
involved.

The second instrument is the pooled per-eye paired test, written
"per eye: $x \pm y$ ($m$/75)". For every validation eye, the growth-region
Dice at the one-year anchor is averaged over the late epochs. The two arms
are paired eye by eye, and the pairs are pooled over the validation eyes of
all five folds: 75 eyes, or 150 eye-seed pairs when an arm was run at two
seeds. Here $m$ is the number of eyes on which the difference has the same
sign as the mean, and the paired $t$ statistic is given where it is
available. Robustness is checked by leaving out one fold at a time. This
instrument uses the growth-region Dice rather than the headline metric,
because the change-region Dice was logged per run as a cohort mean only,
without per-eye values across epochs. This is a limitation of the logging,
not a choice of metric.

Both instruments are always quoted together. A result is established ---
it \emph{graduates} --- only when both agree. The two instruments can
disagree, and \S\ref{sec:experiments:ingredients} contains such a case.
All numbers are for seed 42 unless they are marked as seed 7 or
seed-pooled.

The three highest arms are statistically indistinguishable, and they come
from three unrelated architecture classes: a graph network, a U-Net and a
finite-element network. The dilated-stencil graph network against the U-Net
gives $\Delta = +0.021 \pm 0.017$~SE at seed 42 and only
$+0.008 \pm 0.008$ at seed 7, both under the mixed floor of 0.020. At
seed 7, the graph network reads $0.5279 \pm 0.0419$ and the U-Net 0.5199.
The T-FEN against the dilated-stencil graph network gives
$\Delta = +0.0079 \pm 0.0077$~SE (3/5) and per eye $+0.0050$ (43/75,
$t = 0.79$), seed-pooled about $+0.005$: a null. The T-FEN against the
U-Net gives, seed-pooled, $\Delta = +0.0199 \pm 0.0085$ (8/10 fold-seed
pairs) and per eye $+0.0218 \pm 0.0053$ (97/150). The fold-paired value
sits at the mixed floor, and its minimum when one fold is left out falls
below it, so this pair is also read as indistinguishable. No ordering
among the three is quotable.

The T-FEN has the highest mean in the table, but it should not be read as
the best model. As trained, it is not a valid free-running integrator: its
free-running rollout is unstable on seven of its ten runs (five folds, two
seeds), and nine of its ten final checkpoints exceed the stability bound of
the explicit scheme on at least one channel
(\S\ref{sec:experiments:validity}). Its Dice values at the anchor are
unaffected and stand, but it is not a stable forecasting model in its
current form, and its learned velocity field is not interpreted anywhere
in this thesis.

The per-pixel floor reads exactly 0.0000 on all five folds, identical to
persistence; its collapse counter flags every validation eye as unchanged
from baseline. A model without spatial context predicts no change at all.
This licenses reading every point of change-region Dice in the table as
coming from spatial context.

The fold is a large source of variance. For the $k$-nearest-neighbour graph
network, the fold values range from about 0.41 to 0.52. The standard
deviations in the table are between-fold deviations and are much larger
than the replicate floor, which is why differences are always read
fold-paired. Adjacent rows mostly lie within the floor of each other, so
the table is not a leaderboard. What it provides is the evidence for which
components of an operator move the metric, which
\S\ref{sec:experiments:ingredients} examines one at a time.

The best-fold column shows how far a single fold can lie above the mean.
For every arm except the FNO and the FNO with the Runge--Kutta wrapper,
the best fold is fold 2, so the column mostly reflects the spread between
folds rather than a separate result. The Runge--Kutta arms are nulls against their single-step
counterparts and are taken up in~\S\ref{sec:experiments:ablations}. The
cost column is discussed in~\S\ref{sec:experiments:cost}.


\section{The Ingredient Study}
\label{sec:experiments:ingredients}

The strongest arms of different classes are indistinguishable, so
comparing whole architectures says little about what makes an operator
work. This section therefore compares operators that differ in one
property only, with the rest of the framework held fixed. Each comparison
measures one ingredient. All comparisons are fold-paired over the same five
folds.

\subsection{Spatial Context}
\label{sec:experiments:ingredients:context}

The locality ladder uses the graph network on the index-space
$k$-nearest-neighbour graph with zero, one and two message-passing rounds
(\S\ref{sec:method:family:floors}). With zero rounds, the per-pixel floor
reads 0.0000 on every fold, identical to persistence. With one round, the
one-hop floor reads $0.4529 \pm 0.0403$. With two rounds, the $k$-NN arm
reads $0.4515 \pm 0.0429$. One round against two is a null on the per-eye
instrument (39/75 eyes).

A single ring of neighbours therefore buys everything the graph network on
this graph achieves, and the second ring buys nothing measurable. Every
point of change-region Dice comes from spatial context. One possible reason
why the second ring adds nothing is where its extra reach lies: on the
index-space graph it extends mostly across rows, and only about 0.01\,mm
along them (\S\ref{sec:method:graph}). This motivates the
next comparison, which changes where the neighbours lie.

\subsection{Physical Reach at Lesion Scale}
\label{sec:experiments:ingredients:reach}

This comparison keeps the graph network, the loss and the data, and changes
only the edge set, from the index-space $k$-nearest-neighbour graph to the
dilated stencil (\S\ref{sec:method:graph}). With the same aggregation (mean
and maximum) on both graphs, and therefore the same number of parameters,
the difference is $\Delta = +0.0691 \pm 0.0116$~SE (5/5). The canonical
configuration, the dilated stencil with mean aggregation (67\,147
parameters), against the $k$-NN arm (75\,339 parameters, so at 0.89 times
the parameters) gives $\Delta = +0.0743 \pm 0.0083$~SE (5/5) and per eye
$+0.0752 \pm 0.0054$ (74/75, $t = 13.9$). The pre-committed replication at
seed 7 gives $\Delta = +0.0736 \pm 0.0162$~SE (5/5), and seed-pooled per
eye $+0.0736 \pm 0.0044$ (144/150 eye-seed pairs). This is the largest
effect in the project. It was obtained by changing only the edge set, with
fewer parameters, and it is the reason the dilated stencil is the
canonical configuration.

Table~\ref{tab:experiments:reach} compares other stencil geometries with the
default. Both a shorter (up to $\pm 12$ columns) and a longer (up to
$\pm 42$ columns) reach along the rows are worse by more than the paired
floor, so the default of $\pm 21$ columns, about 0.12\,mm, is a measured
optimum on this axis. It lies close to the 90th percentile of the
per-visit front advance, 23 columns (\S\ref{sec:method:graph}). The
geometry reaching $\pm 35$ columns with an unchanged number of neighbours
is also lower, but under the floor; it shows that the penalty at
$\pm 42$ columns is not a matter of neighbour density alone. Widening the
stencil across rows, to $\pm 2$ rows, costs the most of all geometries.

A higher degree at the same reach buys nothing. The index-space
$k$-nearest-neighbour graph with $k = 20$ reaches as far as with $k = 12$
but has more neighbours. Against the $k = 12$ arm with mean aggregation, it
reads $-0.010 \pm 0.005$ (1/5); against the $k = 12$ arm with mean and
maximum aggregation, $+0.000 \pm 0.005$ (3/5) and per eye $-0.001$ (36/75).
What matters is where the neighbours are placed, not how many there are.

This also explains an earlier result. Before the dilated stencil, the
parameter-matched U-Net was higher than the $k$-NN graph network:
$\Delta = +0.0531 \pm 0.0226$~SE (4/5), per eye $+0.0504 \pm 0.0094$
(55/75, $t = 5.36$), and at seed 7, with the same seed on both sides,
$+0.0655 \pm 0.0209$ fold-paired and $+0.0618 \pm 0.0103$ per eye (60/75).
Fold 3 reads $-0.022$, so the effect is fold-heterogeneous. With the
dilated stencil, the graph network is indistinguishable from the U-Net
(\S\ref{sec:experiments:main-results}). The deficit of the graph network
was therefore located in its graph --- in where its neighbours lie
physically --- and not in its operator class.

\subsection{Global and Local Context Together}
\label{sec:experiments:ingredients:global}

The Fourier Neural Operator has global spectral support but no local path
(\S\ref{sec:method:family:dense}). The local-kernel hybrid adds a
$3 \times 3$ local path to the same operator, at a cost of 800 parameters.

The FNO against the $k$-NN graph network gives
$\Delta = +0.0316 \pm 0.0259$~SE (3/5) and per eye $+0.0225 \pm 0.0142$
(40/75). Fold 4 carries about 77\,\% of it, and it does not survive leaving
one fold out on either instrument, so this difference is not established.
The FNO against the U-Net gives $\Delta = -0.0215 \pm 0.0139$~SE (1/5) and
per eye $-0.0279 \pm 0.0111$ (30/75). That the FNO lies below the U-Net at
the one-year anchor is established. At horizons beyond one year, the order
of the two is reported to differ.

The hybrid against the $k$-NN graph network gives
$\Delta = +0.0470 \pm 0.0160$~SE (5/5) and per eye $+0.0414 \pm 0.0115$
(47/75). It is the only arm that is higher than the $k$-NN graph network on
every fold. The effect of the local path itself is the hybrid against the
FNO: $\Delta = +0.0153 \pm 0.0110$~SE, which is under the paired floor,
but per eye $+0.0190 \pm 0.0070$ (48/75), which graduates on that
instrument. The two instruments disagree here, which is why both are
always quoted. The hybrid against the U-Net gives
$\Delta = -0.0061 \pm 0.0108$~SE and per eye $-0.0089$ (33/75): a tie.

Global spectral support alone therefore does not reproduce the result of
the U-Net, whereas global support with a minimal local path does. Global
context and local detail are separable ingredients, and both are needed.
Two architecturally unrelated constructions that combine them --- the
multiscale pyramid of the U-Net and the FNO with a local path --- arrive at
the same level at matched capacity.

\subsection{An Explicit Transport Term}
\label{sec:experiments:ingredients:transport}

This comparison pairs the T-FEN with the same finite-element network
without its transport head, at the same width
(\S\ref{sec:method:family:fen}). The ablation is purely additive: both
heads are zero-initialised, so both arms start as the same function. The
difference is $\Delta = +0.0527 \pm 0.0065$~SE (5/5) at seed 42 and
$+0.0495 \pm 0.0081$~SE (5/5) at seed 7, and per eye
$+0.0555 \pm 0.0057$ (65/75, $t = 9.68$). The effect is established on
both instruments at both seeds.

This is, however, the only architecture comparison in the survey that is
not parameter-matched: the T-FEN has 68\,503 parameters and the free-form
control 34\,785, a factor of 1.97. A matched free-form control was not run.
Part of the gain may therefore come from the extra parameters. One
observation argues, indirectly, against reading all of it that way: the
effect is concentrated in the near-term growth bin, horizons up to one
year, where it brings the T-FEN to the level of the dilated stencil,
rather than being spread over all horizons as a generic gain from capacity
would be. Settling the question needs the matched control.

\subsection{Two Routes to One Deficit}
\label{sec:experiments:ingredients:convergence}

The GA front moves about 10 to 25 columns per visit, with a median of 12
and a 90th percentile of 23 columns (\S\ref{sec:method:graph}). One hop on
the index-space graph reaches only $\pm 2$ columns, about 0.011\,mm along a
row. An operator of that kind cannot see as far as the lesion front moves
between two visits.

Two unrelated constructions correct this deficit. The first is physical
reach: the dilated stencil, whose single hop spans 21 columns. The second
is an explicit transport term: the T-FEN, with the caveat of its 1.97-fold
parameter gap. Each is established against its own control, and the two
end up indistinguishable from each other and from the U-Net. The central
architectural result of the survey is this coincidence --- two independent
routes to the same deficit --- rather than the score of any single arm. It
is suggestive rather than a proof of mechanism.

The architecture class therefore does not decide the outcome; the
ingredients it carries do. \S\ref{sec:experiments:ablations} reports what
was measured and did not matter.


\section{Negative Results}
\label{sec:experiments:ablations}

A negative result here is a comparison whose difference stays under the
floor of the instruments defined in~\S\ref{sec:experiments:protocol:stats}.
It is a null at the available precision. It does not show that the tested
alternative is equivalent, or that the mechanism behind it has no effect,
and each finding is scoped to the backbone, the folds and the seed on which
it was measured. This section reports two groups of such results: the time
handling of the update (\S\ref{sec:experiments:ablations:time}) and the
composition of the training objective (\S\ref{sec:experiments:ablations:loss}).

\subsection{Time Handling}
\label{sec:experiments:ablations:time}

The residual update of~\S\ref{sec:method:dt-residual} multiplies the output
of the operator by the interval,
$u_{t+\Delta t} = u_t + \Delta t \cdot f_\theta(u_t, \Delta t, z)$. In the
ablation, this outer multiplication was removed, so the update became
$u_t + f_\theta$, while $\Delta t$ was kept as an input feature of the
operator. The comparison was run on the index-space $k$-nearest-neighbour
graph network (\S\ref{sec:method:graph}) over five folds at seed 42.
Removing the scaling gives $\Delta = +0.0134 \pm 0.0059$~SE (4/5) against
the canonical update.
% TODO: insert the per-eye instrument for the Delta t-scaling ablation, if
% it was computed.
This is under the five-fold paired floor of 0.016, so by the pre-registered
rule the change does not graduate, and the canonical update keeps the
multiplication.

The scaling could be removed without a measurable loss because, on the mask
channel, it is a reparameterisation rather than an inductive bias. The mask
is binary, and in normalised units its two values differ by a fixed gap: 0
maps to $-0.602$ and 1 to $+1.660$ (\S\ref{sec:data:normalization}). At a
pixel that changes between two visits, the target of the scaled form,
$(y - u)/\Delta t$, is therefore exactly plus or minus that gap divided by
$\Delta t$, at every interval length. The operator has to produce an
internal division by $\Delta t$, which the outer multiplication then
cancels. The operator is thus a one-step map conditioned on $\Delta t$, not
a rate that can be queried. Because $\Delta t$ remains an input, the
operator receives the same time information with or without the
multiplication, which is why dropping it loses nothing measurable. The
multiplication is kept because it is the form MP-PDE defines
\citep{Brandstetter2022} and because it gives the $\Delta t \to 0$ limit in
which the prediction reduces to persistence (\S\ref{sec:method:dt-residual}).
A reading of the operator as a learned rate is not supported by this
result; \S\ref{sec:experiments:validity:swap} tests it directly.

The integration order is the second test of the time handling. The
Runge--Kutta wrapper (\S\ref{sec:method:family:rk}) was placed around the
canonical dilated-stencil graph network in its autonomous setting, in which
the $\Delta t$ input is set to zero and $\Delta t$ only sets the
integration horizon, and compared with a single Euler step of the same
network. The difference is $\Delta = -0.0119 \pm 0.0037$~SE (1/5), and per
eye $-0.0132 \pm 0.0055$ (28/75): a null, in the direction of the single
step. Over the FNO, the wrapper against the plain FNO gives
$\Delta = +0.0062 \pm 0.0056$~SE (4/5), and per eye $+0.0064 \pm 0.0069$
(43/75), also a null. Raising the integration order therefore gives no
detectable benefit over either a local graph operator or a global spectral
one.

A single-fold decomposition on fold 2 shows where the five-fold difference
comes from. Every step is under the single-fold floor of 0.036, so it is
indicative only. The wrapper at one Euler step reproduces the canonical
operator to $-0.010$; removing the $\Delta t$ input feature, as in the
autonomous setting, costs a further $-0.005$; and raising the order from
Euler to RK4 costs $-0.0002$. Most of the five-fold difference is thus the
wrapper's own offset at the scale of the replicate noise, not the
integration order.

The higher order is expensive. The minimum epoch time is 439\,s for RK4 over
the stencil network against 91\,s for one Euler step, about $4.8\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:experiments:arms}), and 45\,s against 23\,s for the FNO. The
factor of $4.8$ holds where the forward pass of the backbone dominates the
epoch. Finally, the change-region Dice at the anchor is thresholded and
blind to the trajectory between visits, so these nulls license no statement
about continuous-time behaviour; \S\ref{sec:experiments:validity:swap}
addresses that question.

\subsection{The Objective}
\label{sec:experiments:ablations:loss}

The monotonic-growth penalty discourages a decrease of the mask where the
previous state is atrophic (\S\ref{sec:method:training:loss}). It was
measured on fold 2 at seed 42, as an epoch-paired comparison over the 20
late epochs. The run with the penalty at weight 2.0 minus the run without
it reads $-0.0032 \pm 0.0033$~SE, and the penalised run is higher on only 7
of the 20 paired epochs. This is an order of magnitude under the
single-fold floor of 0.036, so the simpler objective was kept, and the
weight is zero in every reported run. Two limits apply. The comparison ran
with the loss configuration used at the time, which did not multiply the
output by $\Delta t$, and the penalty was not re-tested with the final
update. And epoch-paired standard errors are optimistic, because successive
epochs of one run are correlated.

A separate mechanism argues against the penalty under the pushforward
curriculum (\S\ref{sec:method:training:curriculum}). On unrolled steps, the
previous state the penalty refers to is the model's own earlier
prediction, so the penalty locks in the model's own false positives: a
pixel wrongly marked as atrophic can no longer be corrected toward the
healthy ground truth without paying the penalty. On such a pixel, the
gradient of the penalty was measured at 31.8 times the gradient of the data
terms. The penalty would therefore enforce two different priors on
teacher-forced and on unrolled steps. In addition, GA is not constrained to
grow monotonically in this thesis, because the reference segmentations
themselves shrink locally between some visits (\S\ref{sec:method:dt-residual}).

The loss ladder separates the channel weighting from the soft-Dice term. All
of its runs are single runs on fold 2 at seed 42, so the single-fold floor
of 0.036 applies. On the late-epoch change-region Dice, plain unweighted
MSE reads 0.0318: it escapes the collapse in which the model copies its
input only at epoch 26 and does not recover. MSE with the mask channel
weighted by 5 and no soft-Dice term reads 0.4885, and the canonical
objective, which adds the soft-Dice term, reads 0.4963. The last two are
equal within noise. It is therefore the channel weighting, not the
soft-Dice term, that escapes the collapse. What the soft-Dice term adds is
an immediate escape, from epoch 0, and roughly half the epoch-to-epoch
standard deviation of the metric (0.049 to 0.025), not a higher final
score.

The epoch of escape follows the strength of the mask-channel gradient:
epoch 0 at soft-Dice sharpness 2, epoch 2 at sharpness 4, epoch 5 at
sharpness 6, epoch 9 with mask weight 5 and no soft-Dice, and epoch 26 with
plain MSE. Sharpness 2 is kept for its immediate escape and its lowest
epoch-to-epoch variance, not for a higher score. It has a calibration cost.
The raw mask RMSE rises to 0.880 at sharpness 2, against 0.336 without the
soft-Dice term and 0.362 at sharpness 6. These raw values are quoted only
to show the stretch described in~\S\ref{sec:method:training:loss}; it does
not change any thresholded metric. Excluding the padded positions from the
loss was tested on the same ladder, and the single-fold difference was
under the floor.

An intermediate-time-point regulariser was also evaluated. It queried the
model at random intermediate times $\alpha\,\Delta t$, with $\alpha$ drawn
uniformly between 0.1 and 0.9, and penalised the prediction against the
linear interpolation between the two observed visits. At weights 1, 3 and
10, the runs without it matched or beat the regularised runs on
change-region Dice, and it was removed. These runs predate the final
version of the pipeline, so by the rule of~\S\ref{sec:experiments:protocol:rollout}
no number is quoted.

\section{Validity Diagnostics}
\label{sec:experiments:validity}

The two diagnostics of this section are not accuracy numbers. They decide
which statements about the operators are licensed. The first tests whether
a trained operator can be read as a continuous-time system; the second
tests whether the T-FEN, whose Dice scores are among the highest, is a
numerically valid free-running model.

\subsection{The Solver-Swap Test}
\label{sec:experiments:validity:swap}

The solver-swap test asks whether a trained network represents a
continuous-time system. A trained checkpoint is rebuilt from its own
configuration and re-evaluated with the unmodified evaluation code under
several integration schemes and step counts. The wrapper adds no
parameters (\S\ref{sec:method:family:rk}), so every setting applies the
same weights with a different quadrature. As a control, the canonical model
evaluated at one Euler step through the wrapper reproduces its own recorded
epoch-29 change-region Dice exactly (0.596418). Following \citet{Ott2021}
and \citet{Krishnapriyan2023}, a model that represents a continuous-time
system should give nearly the same predictions under schemes of equal or
smaller discretisation error. The test was run after training, on fold 2,
with one seed per model and no retraining.

The first model is the canonical dilated-stencil network, trained with one
Euler step and with the real $\Delta t$ as an input.
Table~\ref{tab:experiments:swap} gives its change-region Dice at the
anchor, the difference to the trained setting, and the raw mask RMSE, which
is used only to follow the trajectory within one model and never to compare
models. The Dice spreads by 0.0856 across the five settings, 2.4 times the
single-fold floor of 0.036 and 6.7 times the replicate standard deviation of
0.0127. It does not converge: successive Euler refinements change the
result by $-0.0162$ and then by a further $-0.0596$, so the change grows by
a factor of 3.7 when the step is halved again, and the order of the schemes
is not monotone (RK4 with one step lies above the trained setting, RK4 with
two steps below it). The canonical model fails the test. It is a
$\Delta t$-conditioned one-step map, not a discretisation of a
continuous-time system. For a step-conditioned model the test cannot be
posed cleanly, because changing the number of steps changes the $\Delta t$
the operator is conditioned on, so the failure mixes refinement of the
integration with conditioning outside the training range. That is itself
the point of the test: an operator whose update depends on the step it is
about to take does not define a continuous system to refine.

The second model is the network trained inside the autonomous RK4 wrapper,
with one RK4 step and the $\Delta t$ input always zero, so that only the
integrator carries time. It is the one checkpoint for which the test is
properly posed. Its four refined settings (Euler with two and four steps,
RK4 with two and four steps) agree to within 0.0044, below the replicate
standard deviation. The Euler sequence converges, with successive
differences of $+0.0179$ and then $+0.0044$, a ratio of 4.05, and the mask
RMSE falls monotonically and flattens, so the trajectory converges as well
as the endpoint. The only outlier is Euler with one step, the crudest
scheme over the full interval, which lies 0.020 low, as a first-order step
is expected to. This model passes the test: its field is
solver-independent. The two models' fold-2 Dice values (0.5964 and 0.5667)
are not a comparison between them, since the first belongs to one
quadrature and the second is the value the model converges to. Two
variants were not run: the canonical model with its $\Delta t$ input set to
zero, which would be far outside its training distribution, and a
five-fold version of the test.

Together, the two results restrict the vocabulary. No statement about
continuous-time behaviour, ordinary differential equations, rate fields or
learned dynamics is licensed for the canonical model or for any headline
result. A continuous-time reading is licensed for the RK4-trained arm only,
on fold 2 and one seed, and that arm is a five-fold null in accuracy against
the canonical model ($-0.0119 \pm 0.0037$~SE,
\S\ref{sec:experiments:ablations:time}) at about $4.8\times$ the training
cost. On this task the continuous-time property is real and obtainable,
and it buys nothing in accuracy; the difficulty lies in spatial reach
(\S\ref{sec:experiments:ingredients:reach}), not in the time formulation.

\subsection{Numerical Stability of the T-FEN}
\label{sec:experiments:validity:tfen}

The stability of the T-FEN (\S\ref{sec:method:family:fen}) was assessed on
its ten runs, five folds at two seeds. A run counts as stable if both its
raw mask RMSE and its raw layer RMSE in the free-running validation rollout
stay at or below 5 on at least 19 of its 20 late epochs. RMSE is used here
only to detect divergence within one arm. The criterion was fixed for
follow-up runs and is applied to these runs retrospectively.
Table~\ref{tab:experiments:courant} lists the runs. Seven of the ten fail
the criterion and three pass. Four of the seven diverge heavily on the mask
channel, on 19, 15, 14 and 8 of the 20 late epochs; the other three fail
mainly through the layer channels. The largest late-epoch mask RMSE reaches
24\,418.

The instability comes from the transport term: the free-form FEN, which
differs only by its absence, never exceeds a mask RMSE of 1.43, and neither
it nor the dilated-stencil network has a late epoch above the threshold on
any fold. It builds up over the rollout. The teacher-forced one-step error
of every T-FEN run lies within $\pm 0.02$ of the free-form FEN on the same
fold, while the validation rollout reaches about 44 RK4 sub-steps at 45
days, against about 8 within the 360-day training curriculum
(\S\ref{sec:method:training:curriculum}). The thresholded metrics do not see
it. On the heavily diverging runs, the predicted lesion area stays at 0.64
to 1.00 of the true area (median per horizon bin), and the change-region
Dice at the anchor is about the same on diverging and clean epochs of one
run (0.529 against 0.513 in one example). Every Dice number
in~\S\ref{sec:experiments:main-results} and~\S\ref{sec:experiments:ingredients}
therefore stands.

A Courant read-back points to the learned velocities. The runs were judged
against a bound of 2.8, the stability limit $2\sqrt{2}$ of RK4 on the
imaginary axis, in the sense of the stability condition of
\citet{Courant1928}. The combined column and row Courant number was read per
channel on all ten final checkpoints. Nine of the ten exceed 2.8 on at
least one channel, with values from 3.16 to 5.71, while nine of the ten
best checkpoints do not; the velocities grow past the stable region as
training continues. Exceedance on a layer channel coincides with
divergence on all six runs where it occurs, and the one run that stays
under the bound on every channel is stable. A plausible reading, not
tested, is that the near-binary mask is flat away from the lesion edge, so
a fast mask velocity mostly acts on a zero gradient, whereas the layer
channels have texture everywhere.

The bound of 2.8 is itself too loose. The assembled transport stencil has a
spectral radius of about 1.17 times the velocity in grid units, so the
correct bound is about 2.4; against it, three further mask-channel
readings in Table~\ref{tab:experiments:courant} fall on the wrong side. This
settles the open value in~\S\ref{sec:method:family:fen}. An earlier check,
made before the five-fold runs, used one fold and the mask channel only and
did not detect the problem; a stability check for a multi-channel transport
operator has to cover every advected channel, both velocity components, the
final as well as the best checkpoint, and more than one fold and seed. A
smaller step does not fix it either. At a 30-day step on fold 0, every
channel falls back under the bound of 2.8 used during the runs (maximum
2.42), and the free-running rollout still diverges, so the Courant
condition is necessary but not sufficient. The principled remedy, a
velocity head whose parameterisation bounds the speed, was deliberately not
implemented; the instability is reported as a finding.

Four consequences follow. The T-FEN as trained is not a numerically valid
long-horizon integrator. Its learned velocity field and its raw fields are
not quotable. Its Dice results at the anchor and its transport effect
(\S\ref{sec:experiments:ingredients:transport}) stand, because they are
measured on thresholded metrics that the divergence does not reach. And a
learned advection velocity is a usable solver component only if its
parameterisation respects the stability bound on every advected channel.
Separately, the FEN family shows a long-horizon deficit that is not caused
by the instability: the free-form FEN, which never diverges, also
under-predicts long-horizon lesion area and lies 0.134 below the
dilated-stencil network in the late growth-region Dice beyond three years
(0.507 against 0.641).
